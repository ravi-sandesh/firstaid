THE CRITICAL MISSION

Why Every High School Needs a First Aid Club

In a sudden medical emergency, the person right beside you is the true first responder. High school students spend over 35 hours a week together on campuses, sports fields, and buses—making them the first line of defense.

4 Minutes

The Golden Window of Survival

Irreversible brain damage starts just 4 minutes after sudden cardiac arrest or airway blockage, yet standard ambulance response times average 8 to 14 minutes. Bystander CPR instantly bridges that fatal gap.

Doubles or triples survival rates
70%+

Emergencies Happen at Home & School

More than 70% of out-of-hospital cardiac emergencies and accidents occur in homes or public community spaces. When high schoolers learn first aid, they safeguard their siblings, parents, teachers, and friends.

Direct family and community protection
Zero Panic

Transforming Fear Into Calm Leadership

Most people freeze during an injury because they simply don't know what to do. The club provides hands-on muscle memory so teens react with composure, lead bystander crowd control, and direct paramedics effectively.

Lifelong crisis management skills
< 3 Min

Severe Bleeding & Trauma Readiness

From lab spills and woodshop accidents to sports collisions and cuts, severe arterial bleeding can lead to death within minutes. Students learn firm wound packing, pressure points, and modern tourniquet application.

Accident and shop/sports safety
Healthcare

Pathways to Pre-Med & Public Service

Club members gain invaluable hands-on medical exposure, peer coaching experience, CPR/First Aid certifications, and community service hours that form standout foundations for future doctors, nurses, EMTs, and leaders.

Real-world pre-health preparation
Campus-Wide

Safer Schools & AED Mapping

When schools have active student First Aid clubs, campus AED locations are audited, first aid kits are kept replenished, and drills are organized so the whole student body becomes conscious and prepared.

Empowering the entire campus ecosystem

GOLDEN RULE: CALL 911 / 112 FIRST • PUSH HARD & FAST

Hands-Only CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

1
Check Scene & Responsiveness

Tap shoulders firmly and shout: "Are you okay?" Look for normal breathing. If no breathing or only gasping, immediately point to someone: "Call 911 and bring the AED!"

2
Hand Placement & Body Posture

Place heel of one hand in the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone). Interlock fingers with other hand. Lock elbows and position shoulders directly over hands.

3
Compress 100-120 Times per Minute

Compress chest at least 2 inches deep (5 cm). Allow chest to fully recoil between each pump. Don't stop until AED arrives or paramedics take over!

Live CPR Rhythm Clicker

Match compressions to 110 beats/min (Tempo of "Stayin' Alive"). Tap to toggle audio metronome!

AED Voice-Guided Operation

1
Turn On Immediately

Open the lid or press the green Power button. The AED will immediately start talking to you in spoken voice prompts.

2
Expose Chest & Stick Pads

Wipe chest dry. Peel backing: place one pad on upper right chest below collarbone, and the second pad on the lower left ribcage below armpit.

3
Stand Clear & Follow Prompts

Shout: "CLEAR!" Don't touch victim while AED analyzes rhythm. If shock is advised, ensure nobody is touching the person, press the blinking Shock button, then immediately resume CPR.

STOP THE BLEED IN 3 STEPS

Severe Traumatic Bleeding Control

1
Direct Hard Pressure

Apply firm, relentless pressure directly over bleeding site using clean gauze, cloth, or your hands if nothing else is available.

2
Wound Packing

For deep open wounds in the groin or armpit, pack sterile gauze tightly into the wound to seal the bleeding vessel, then maintain pressure.

3
Tourniquet on Limbs

For spurting limb bleeding, place a commercial tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound (not over a joint). Tighten the windlass rod until bright red bleeding completely stops, then secure the clip and record time.

🩸
Key Rule: Never Loosen a Tourniquet

Once applied, only trained hospital surgeons should remove a tourniquet. Always note down the exact time of application on the victim's forehead or strap (e.g. T: 14:35).

AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION PROTOCOL

Heimlich Maneuver (Abdominal Thrusts)

1
Assess Severity

If victim is coughing forcefully, encourage them to keep coughing. If they cannot speak, breathe, or make sounds and clutch their throat, intervene immediately!

2
Stance & Fist Position

Stand behind the person, wrap arms around their waist. Make a fist with thumb side against their abdomen, just above the navel and below the rib cage.

3
Inward & Upward Quick Thrusts

Grasp fist with your other hand. Perform quick, forceful upward and inward thrusts until the object pops out or the person becomes unresponsive (if unresponsive, start CPR immediately).

🗣️
Universal Sign of Choking

Two hands crossed over the neck. When you see this, act immediately—a person has only minutes before losing consciousness.

CAMPUS & SPORTS INJURIES

Burns (Cool Water) & Sprains (R.I.C.E.)

1
Burns: Cool with Running Water (10-20 Mins)

Cool thermal or chemical burns under cool running tap water. Never apply ice, butter, or oil, which traps heat. Cover loosely with sterile non-stick bandage.

2
Sprains & Strains: Follow R.I.C.E.

Rest the injured joint. Ice for 15-20 mins with towel barrier. Compress with elastic wrap (not too tight). Elevate above heart level.

3
Concussion Check in Sports

Look for dizziness, confusion, nausea, light sensitivity, or pupil inequality. When in doubt: "Sit Them Out!"

🧊
The Golden Burn Rule

Cool, don't freeze! Never put freezing ice on burn blisters; it damages skin tissue. Clean cool water is the gold standard.
